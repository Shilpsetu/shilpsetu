Position

Reading the brief

The problem statement names three features — an image enhancer, a multilingual auto-cataloger, a pricing assistant. Every team that picks 26090 will build those three and demo them. Three observations decide whether we place.

The title puts market linkage first

"AI-Driven Market Linkage and Smart Cataloging." The stated impact goal is a continuous, year-round digital sales channel, reducing dependency on periodic physical fairs. A beautiful catalog that no buyer ever sees is a photo album. The three named features are the on-ramp; the road is ONDC and GeM. We build the on-ramp properly, then we actually connect it to demand — which is the half most teams will gesture at on a slide and never implement.

The binding constraint is literacy, not AI

Background removal is a solved problem you can run on a phone. What is not solved is a 45-year-old weaver with a ₹7,000 Android device, patchy 4G, and no reading fluency completing a product listing without help. That is an interaction-design problem, and it is where the 25% novelty weight is genuinely available — it is also the thing a judge can watch happen in thirty seconds.

The Dataset Link is empty

The problem statement supplies no data. Any team promising a trained market-price model is either hand-waving or has overfit scraped noise, and a probing judge will find the seam. We design a pricing system that is defensible without a dataset we do not have — and turn that honesty into the strongest line on the impact slide.

And the fourth, which decides the pitch

The Ministry has already built the marketplace. Bharat TULIP, launched by MoSJE through NBCFDC in November 2024, runs a dedicated Amazon.in storefront, Mystore.in listings, Heritage Collection Stores and an annual Seller–Buyer Meet, with products sorted into four curated collections — Virasat Living, Ratna Rekha, Resham Sutra and Leather Legacy.

It has served roughly 1,500 artisans. The same ministry reports ₹17,225 crore disbursed to 54 lakh beneficiaries. That ratio is the entire problem statement in one number.

Any team that walks in saying "artisans have no digital channel, so we will build one" is telling a MoSJE judge that they did not read the ministry's own press releases. The channel exists. What does not exist is a way onto it — because getting a listing onto an Amazon shelf requires a photographer, a copywriter and a pricing analyst, which is precisely the list of things the problem statement says these artisans lack.

Thesis

Karigar is not a marketplace, and not a cataloging app with AI bolted on. It is the on-ramp to the market the Ministry already built — an entry barrier driven to zero: one photo, one spoken sentence, no typing, no reading, no signal required.

We are not asking MoSJE to adopt a new platform. We are telling them why TULIP has 1,500 artisans instead of 50,000, and handing them the fix.

Four bets

These are the claims we defend on stage. Everything in the build plan exists to make one of them true.

Bet 01 · Interaction

Zero-literacy by construction

No typing anywhere on the critical path. Products are identified by their own photograph, never by a typed name. Every screen speaks its prompt in the artisan's language and every confirmation is read back aloud. Price confirmation uses currency-note imagery, not digits alone.

The acceptance test is blunt: a person who cannot read completes a full listing unaided.

Bet 02 · Model design

Craft-aware, not craft-blind

A generic segmenter treats a Pattachitra scroll like a sneaker. We ship a GI-anchored craft taxonomy — Pochampally ikat, Madhubani, Bidriware, Channapatna, Kutch embroidery, Bhagalpuri silk — and an on-device classifier that identifies the craft first.

The craft class then conditions everything downstream: segmentation strategy, description vocabulary, SEO keywords, and which comparables the pricing engine is allowed to look at. Generic AI writes "blue cloth."

Bet 03 · Economics

A wage floor, not a black box

We do not output one confident number. We output a floor derived from materials plus hours at the state handloom fair-wage rate, and a market band from craft-matched comparables — and we show the artisan the gap between the two.

The app will not suggest a price below the floor, and when the floor sits above the whole market band it says so out loud rather than hiding it. On a Social Justice problem statement, an app that refuses to let an artisan underprice their own labour is the impact slide.

Bet 04 · Distribution

Feed the channel that already exists

The catalog is authored once and exported everywhere. Bharat TULIP first — mapped to the ministry's own four collections, with images built to the Amazon listing specification TULIP's storefront actually enforces. Then ONDC seller network, GeM-ready bundles, and a B2B buyer portal with semantic search.

The loop closes when a bulk enquiry arrives back in the artisan's app as a spoken alert in their own language — not an English email they cannot read.

Supporting: the craft passport

Every listing carries a scannable provenance record — cluster, GI tag, loom type, and the artisan's existing government identity rather than one we invent. The Pehchan artisan ID card and the NBCFDC beneficiary record already exist; we reference them and let the ministry's own registry be the source of truth. Verified authenticity is what commands a price premium for genuine handloom, and it costs us one table and a QR encoder.

Design

System architecture

Three bands: what runs on a phone that may have no signal, what runs on our servers, and where the demand lives. The edge worth staring at is the one coming back from right to left.

Decisions on record

Six architectural decisions, each with the reason we will be asked for. These go into docs/adr/ as the first commits so nobody relitigates them in week four.

ADR-01Offline-first, with the device as UI source of truth

The local Drift database backs every screen; writes go to an outbox with an idempotency key and reconcile server-side on reconnect. Because craft clusters have intermittent 4G, and an artisan who photographs twenty products in a courtyard with no bars must not lose twenty products. Cost: real conflict-resolution work in Phase 2. Accepted.

ADR-02Every AI capability sits behind a provider adapter

One interface per capability — SpeechTranscriber, Translator, DescriptionWriter, ImageEnhancer, PriceEstimator. Implementations: Bhashini, self-hosted, and Mock.

Because it buys three things at once: no vendor lock-in for a government deployment, a credible sovereign-stack answer when a judge asks about recurring cost, and a fully offline demo mode. The mock provider is not a testing convenience — it is our insurance against the venue wifi.

ADR-03Split the image pipeline across the boundary

Segmentation, crop and white-balance run on-device and return in under a second. Super-resolution and relighting queue for the server. Because the artisan must see a clean cut-out immediately — that instant feedback is what makes the app feel like magic — while the expensive pass can land minutes later without anyone waiting on it.

ADR-04Bhashini as primary Indic language provider

ASR, translation and TTS route through Bhashini (the Digital India Bhashini Division's ULCA APIs) with self-hosted Whisper and IndicTrans2 behind the same adapter. Because it is the Government of India's own national language stack, covers the 22 scheduled languages, and on a ministry problem statement "we built on Bhashini" is a materially better answer than "we call a US API."

ADR-05Contract-first: OpenAPI generates the Dart client

FastAPI emits the schema; CI regenerates the typed Dart client and fails the build on drift. Because with six people and two tiers, the default failure mode is the app team blocked on the backend team. Generated clients plus mock servers mean neither side ever waits.

ADR-06One language per tier — Python everywhere on the server

No Node service alongside the Python ML code. Because six students under deadline can hold one server mental model, one dependency manager and one test runner. The ML people and the API people share tooling instead of negotiating a boundary. Cost: we give up Node's ecosystem for the buyer portal's backend. Accepted.

The listing pipeline

Two inputs — one photograph, one spoken sentence — converge into a listing. The interesting design work is which steps sit on which side of the network boundary, because that choice is what makes the app usable in a village and cheap to run at scale.

What the extraction step actually produces

The transcript is never handed to the description model as free text. It is parsed into a typed schema first — material, technique, dimensions, colours, hours to make, quantity available — so that missing fields become spoken follow-up questions rather than model hallucinations.

The pricing engine

The problem statement asks for a price "based on current market trends and raw material costs" and supplies no dataset. Here is how we honour the ask without inventing a model we cannot defend.

Inputs

  • From the image — craft class, and a finish-quality score (weave regularity, edge finish, colour saturation) that positions the item within its band.
  • From the voice intake — three spoken questions: what materials, roughly how many days, how many do you have. Nothing typed.
  • From the comparables index — a craft-partitioned price index we seed from public marketplace listings, exhibition price lists and GeM catalog data, refreshed on a schedule and versioned so a suggestion can always be traced to the snapshot that produced it.

Output

A floor, a suggested price, a stretch price, and a plain-language spoken rationale: "Your materials and eleven days of work come to ₹1,850. Sarees like yours sell between ₹2,400 and ₹3,200. I suggest ₹2,700." Every number the artisan hears can be explained back to them, which is the whole point.

When the floor is above the market

Running the implementation end to end surfaced the case that turns out to matter most. A Pochampally saree takes around 88 hours. At the state fair-wage rate that is a floor near ₹6,550 — while comparable sarees list at ₹2,400–₹3,200. The floor sits above the entire market band.

The tempting fix is to clamp the band up to the floor. It is also wrong: it produces the spoken sentence "items like yours sell between ₹6,552 and ₹6,552", and, worse, it conceals the finding. So the engine treats this as a first-class outcome. It reports the true band, prices at the floor, and says the hard thing plainly:

Spoken aloud, in her language

"Your materials and eleven days of work come to 6,552 rupees. Items like yours are selling for only 2,400 to 3,200 rupees. That is less than your work is worth. I have set your price at 6,552 rupees. It may sell slowly, but it will not sell at a loss."

This is the strongest thing in the build. For one artisan it is a decision she can now make with the numbers in front of her. Aggregated across a cluster it is evidence — a measurement of exactly how far below fair wage a craft has been pushed, in a form the Ministry can act on. No team that ships a single confident price can produce it.

Engineering standards

Six people writing code for four months under exam pressure. Standards are not ceremony here — they are the only thing that keeps the last fortnight from being a merge crisis.

Flutter

very_good_analysis lints, zero warnings on main. Riverpod v2 with codegen for state, freezed for models, Drift for persistence, go_router for navigation, mocktail for tests. Golden tests on the six core screens — they catch the layout regressions that a low-end device will otherwise find for us on stage.

Python

uv for dependencies, ruff for lint and format, mypy in strict mode, pytest with a real Postgres in CI via testcontainers. Alembic for every schema change — no manual DDL, ever. Domain layer imports nothing from FastAPI.

Git

Trunk-based. Branches live under 48 hours. Conventional commits. Every PR needs one review and green CI; main is always demoable, because at some point someone will ask to see it with no notice.

Definition of done

Merged, tested, lint-clean, works offline where the feature claims to, has a Hindi string in the ARB file, and has been run once on the ₹7,000 reference device. Not "works on my emulator."

Buy the reference device in week one

A 2 GB-RAM Android phone, bought early and kept on the desk. Every performance claim we make gets checked against it. Teams that only ever test on a flagship discover their ML pipeline is unusable in December, when there is no time left to fix it.

Prototype scope — what Phase 1 must do

Written as acceptance criteria so there is no argument on 14 September about whether we are done.

  • An artisan opens the app and reaches the camera in one tap, with a spoken prompt in Hindi.
  • The quality gate refuses a blurred or backlit frame before the shutter fires, and says why aloud.
  • Segmentation returns a clean cut-out on a white ground in under 1.5 seconds on the reference device.
  • A voice note in Hindi becomes a structured attribute set, with missing fields asked back as spoken questions.
  • English and Hindi descriptions generate with craft-appropriate vocabulary, not generic adjectives.
  • A price floor, suggestion and stretch appear with a spoken rationale the artisan can follow.
  • The artisan hears the description read back and approves or re-records — no typing occurs anywhere.
  • The listing appears in the buyer portal within five seconds of approval.
  • The whole flow runs against mock providers with the network disabled.

Explicitly out of Phase 1: offline sync, the trained classifier, languages beyond Hindi, real comparables, auth, ONDC, orders. Every one of those is Phase 2 or later, and pulling any of them forward is how the skeleton fails to ship.
